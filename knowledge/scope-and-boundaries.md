# Scope and boundaries

Coaching complements, but never replaces, medical assessment or treatment. Stating this clearly on the site builds trust with scientifically minded visitors.

## Within scope

Lifestyle habits; movement and general fitness; mobility; stress-regulation practices; sleep-supportive behaviours; meditation and relaxation; body awareness; pelvic-awareness education within training and competence; accountability and implementation.

## Outside scope

Diagnosing or treating any condition, including pelvic floor dysfunction or erectile dysfunction; prescribing rehabilitation; hormone or medication advice; psychotherapy; promising specific physical or sexual outcomes.

## Refer to a clinician when a client reports

- Sudden or persistent changes in sexual function
- Chest pain, breathlessness or unusual exercise intolerance
- Severe pelvic pain or pain during sex
- Blood in urine or semen, or unexplained genital bleeding
- Significant urinary symptoms
- Suspected sleep apnoea, or persistent insomnia
- Persistent unexplained fatigue
- Depression, severe anxiety or thoughts of self-harm
- Medication or hormone questions

## Breathwork safety

Hyperventilation and breath-hold practices are never done in water, while driving, or anywhere that loss of consciousness could cause injury. They are not standard practice for every client.

## Suggested site wording

> This is coaching, not medical care. If something needs a doctor, I'll tell you, and our work will sit alongside theirs.
