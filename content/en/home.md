## Approach

**Heading:** Three rules I coach by

### Start smaller than feels useful
A five-minute practice you do on your worst day beats an hour you skip. We build up from whatever survives.

### Every practice comes with its reason
No belief system required. You'll know what's well supported, what's plausible, and what's simply worth trying.

### The goal is to not need me
By the end you run your own practice, including the plan for travel, illness and bad weeks.

## Pillars

**Heading:** Six areas we draw on

**Intro:** You won't work on all six at once. We start where it matters most for you, and add the rest when the basics hold.

### Breathe
**Description:** Slower breathing you can use anywhere: before a meeting, in traffic, at 3am.
**Example practice:** Four seconds in, six seconds out, for one minute.
**Why:** Longer exhales tend to shift the body toward rest, which is why we usually start here.

### Move
**Description:** Mobility, strength and yoga for a body that sits all day.
**Example practice:** A ten-minute floor routine before your first coffee.
**Why:** For stiff hips and backs, a little every day tends to do more than a long session once a week.

### Focus
**Description:** Meditation and attention training, with no incense required.
**Example practice:** Three minutes counting breaths at your desk, eyes open.
**Why:** Short, regular practice is easier to keep than long sessions, and the keeping is what counts.

### Restore
**Description:** Sleep, deep rest and recovery, including Yoga Nidra.
**Example practice:** A guided Yoga Nidra recording for the evenings your mind won't switch off.
**Why:** It gives a busy mind something to follow instead of something to fight.

### Vitality
**Description:** Energy, confidence in your body, and sexual wellbeing, talked about privately, plainly and within coaching scope.
**Example practice:** Pelvic-floor awareness built into your movement routine.
**Why:** It's part of the same body you train everywhere else, so it belongs in the same routine. Anything clinical goes to a doctor.

### Grow
**Description:** Habits that last, and a practice that's yours.
**Example practice:** A ten-minute weekly review: what worked, what didn't, what we change.
**Why:** Small changes every week keep a routine alive when your life changes around it.

## FAQ

**Heading:** Questions people ask

### Is this medical treatment?
No. This is coaching, not medical care. If something needs a doctor, I'll tell you, and our work will sit alongside theirs.

### I don't have time.
That's where we start. The first routine is the smallest one that survives your busiest week, sometimes five minutes. We add more only when it holds.

### I'm sceptical of yoga and breathwork.
Good. You don't need to believe anything. Every practice comes with its reasoning, and I'll tell you which parts are well supported, which are plausible, and which are simply experience.

### I already know what I should be doing.
Most people do. The work is in doing it consistently, in your actual week, and adjusting when that week changes.

### How often do we meet?
It depends on you. We set the rhythm of sessions around your situation and what you need, and change it as you go. Between sessions, you get in touch whenever you need to, and you'll have routines, short videos and guided recordings to work with.

### How long is the programme, and what does it cost?
Three to six months, depending on your goals. Investment is discussed on our call, once we know what you need.

### What happens on the first call?
It's free and takes thirty minutes on video. We talk about what you want, what's getting in the way, and whether anything should go to a doctor first. If it looks like a fit, I'll email you a written plan and the investment within two days. There's no pressure on the call.

### Which languages, and where do I need to be?
Sessions are in English or Spanish, online, wherever you live. I offer morning and evening times so most time zones can find a slot.
